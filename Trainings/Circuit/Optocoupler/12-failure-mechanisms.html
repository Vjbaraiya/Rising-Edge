MODULE 12

Failure Mechanisms

How optocouplers die: LED light-output degradation kinetics, CTR drift signatures in field data, thermal and moisture stress paths, ESD damage patterns, and the rare but serious isolation barrier breakdown — with screening and prevention strategies for each.

Learning Objectives

After completing this module, you will be able to:

Recognize CTR degradation signatures in returned units
Assess thermal, moisture, and ESD stress risks
Analyze isolation breakdown causes and precursors
Define screening and prevention measures
Where this fits. Module 9 showed how to design with margin against ageing. This module looks at what actually goes wrong in the field: each mechanism, the symptom it leaves behind, how to tell mechanisms apart on a returned unit, and how to prevent them. It builds on Module 3 (construction) and prepares for Module 13 (testing).

Optocouplers fail in a small number of well-understood ways. Most are slow drifts (LED ageing, leakage growth) that a margin-based design absorbs. A few are sudden (reverse breakdown, cracked packages). One, barrier breakdown, is a safety failure that must be prevented by design rather than detected in service.

RPN (risk priority number) is a ranking aid, not an absolute measure. Many organisations now also use action priority (AP) tables (AIAG–VDA FMEA), where high severity demands action regardless of RPN.

The infrared LED is the only component that wears out under normal operation. Its light output falls because non-radiative recombination centres (crystal defects) multiply during operation:

Defect growth

Dislocations and point defects in and near the active layer grow, fed by recombination energy and current. Each defect is a site where carriers recombine without emitting light.

Current density

More carriers per unit area means faster defect generation: degradation rises super-linearly with IF.

Temperature

Defect migration is thermally activated (Arrhenius). Junction temperature includes the LED’s own heating.

Low-current sensitivity

Non-radiative losses matter most at low current, so an aged LED loses proportionally more light at low IF.

Rate of light loss ∝ Jn · exp(−Ea/kTJ)    (J = current density, n > 1; see Module 9 for the time dependence)
Pro tip AlGaAs and GaAs:Si LEDs age differently, and vendors’ processes differ even more. Use each vendor’s own degradation data; do not transfer curves between manufacturers of “the same” part number.

Design trap — assuming normal CTR means a healthy barrier. Barrier damage is electrically invisible in CTR. Only insulation-resistance, PD or hi-pot tests reveal it, and repeated hi-pot testing can itself cause damage.

Temperature swings stress the package mechanically because silicon, GaAs, copper lead frames, gold wires and epoxy all expand at different rates:

Wire-bond heel fatigue

The bond wire flexes each cycle; cracks grow at the heel until the wire opens. First symptom: temperature-dependent intermittents.

Die-attach fatigue

Voids and cracks in the die attach raise thermal resistance, which raises TJ and accelerates LED ageing.

Package cracking

Large ΔT or thermal shock cracks the mould, opening paths for moisture.

Mitigation

Qualified parts (JEDEC/AEC temperature cycling), reduced ΔT (placement away from heat sources), conformal coating that does not stress the package.

Mould compounds absorb water. Moisture causes two separate problems:

Popcorning in reflow

Absorbed moisture flashes to steam at reflow temperature and delaminates or cracks the package. Controlled by the MSL rating, dry-pack, floor-life tracking and baking (J-STD-020/033).

Leakage and ion migration in service

Humidity plus ionic contamination (flux residue, salt) raises surface leakage and dark current; under bias, metal ions can migrate between pins (dendrites).

Worked example — floor life
An MSL-3 part has a floor life of 168 h at ≤ 30 °C / 60 % RH (J-STD-033 representative). A reel sat open for two weeks before a rework campaign.
  1. Two weeks = 336 h > 168 h → the parts must be baked before reflow (per J-STD-033 tables for package thickness).
  2. Skipping the bake risks cracks and delamination that pass functional test but weaken the barrier.

Electrostatic discharge and electrical overstress hit both sides of the coupler:

Design trap — latent ESD damage. A part that survived an ESD event can pass test and fail months later. If an ESD incident is suspected in production, quarantine and screen (leakage, CTR at low IF) rather than ship.

Barrier failure is rare but hazardous: it can put mains voltage on touchable circuits. It almost always develops over time from one of four precursors:

Partial discharge & treeing

Voids or delaminated interfaces discharge each half-cycle once the local field exceeds inception. Erosion channels (trees) grow until they bridge the barrier.

Surface tracking

Contamination plus humidity on too-short creepage paths forms carbon tracks between pins (external) or along internal interfaces.

Overvoltage events

Surges beyond VIOSM or sustained operation above VIORM punch through or accelerate PD.

Mechanical damage

Cracks from popcorning, thermal shock or board flex create new discharge paths.

Part D — Engineering Scenarios

Scenario 1 — The field failures that only happen in winter
Units in unheated warehouses fail intermittently at night and recover during the day.
Analysis. Cold-dependent intermittents suggest mechanical opens (bond heel cracks, die-attach cracks) that close when warm, or condensation leakage at dawn. Measure at −20 °C, X-ray the bonds and check for condensation residue. Fix: qualified parts for the temperature range, reduce thermal cycling amplitude, conformal coating against condensation.
Scenario 2 — A good CTR, a bad barrier
A returned power-supply board has a coupler with normal CTR but a customer reported a tingle from the enclosure. What do you test, and why is CTR irrelevant?
Analysis. CTR measures the optical path, not the insulation. Test RIO at 500 V DC and, under controlled conditions, PD/hi-pot at a reduced level. Inspect for tracking between pins and cracks. Review the working voltage vs VIORM and the creepage for the real pollution degree. This is a safety investigation: quarantine the lot.
Scenario 3 — Rework campaign aftermath
After a large rework campaign, CTR looks fine but RIO on sampled parts is 100× lower than on untouched boards. Parts had been out of dry-pack for weeks.
Analysis. Likely moisture-induced delamination (popcorning) during rework heating, creating internal paths and PD sites. Confirm with C-SAM. Fix: bake per J-STD-033 before rework, control floor life, and consider replacing the reworked couplers because barrier integrity cannot be proven by a functional test.
