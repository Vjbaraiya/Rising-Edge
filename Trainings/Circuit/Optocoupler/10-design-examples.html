Worst case: IF,min from Vdrive,min, VF,max, Rmax  ·  CTREOL = CTRmin·Πk  ·  k = CTREOL·IF,min / IC,needed ≥ 2

Requirements

  • Isolate a 5 V logic signal from a remote domain into a 3.3 V MCU
  • Signal up to 5 kHz; industrial ambient to 70 °C; 10-year life
  • Functional isolation, standard creepage

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. IF,min = (5.0 − 0.4 − 1.4) / 390 Ω ≈ 8.2 mA (driver drop 0.4 V, VF,max 1.4 V). IF,max ≈ (5.25 − 1.0)/390 ≈ 10.9 mA: fine for life.
  2. IC,needed = (3.3 − 0.2)/4.7 kΩ ≈ 0.66 mA.
  3. CTREOL = 200 % (rank C) × 0.9 × 0.8 × 0.5 = 72 %; IC,avail = 8.2 × 0.72 ≈ 5.9 mA → k ≈ 9.
  4. Speed: τ ≈ 1.5 µs + 250 × 10 pF × 4.7 kΩ ≈ 13 µs → tr ≈ 30 µs: fine for 5 kHz. Deep overdrive adds a few µs of storage; acceptable here.

Common mistakes

Designing with typical CTR

300 % “typical” hides a 50 % unranked minimum.

Huge pull-up to “save current”

47 kΩ makes edges > 200 µs.

Floating LED drive at reset

A tri-stated remote pin can leave the LED half-on.

Test and verification

  • Measure ton/toff at max temperature
  • Reduce IF to 50 % and confirm the output still saturates (EOL simulation)
  • Check VOL ≤ VIL of the MCU at max IC

Requirements

  • 3.3 V MCU switches a 24 V, 20 mA relay coil on an isolated 24 V supply
  • Relay on/off at ≤ 10 Hz; fail-safe OFF at MCU reset

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. IF,min = (3.3 − 0.3 − 1.4)/220 Ω ≈ 7.3 mA; check the GPIO current rating (typ. 8–20 mA per pin).
  2. Pull-up 10 kΩ to 24 V → IC,needed = 2.4 mA. Rank B at EOL: 7.3 × 1.3 × 0.36 ≈ 3.4 mA (k ≈ 1.4: marginal). Rank C: ≈ 5.3 mA (k ≈ 2.2).
  3. Gate: 24 V exceeds a typical ±20 V VGS,max. Use a 10 kΩ/10 kΩ divider (12 V gate) or a 15 V zener clamp.
  4. Coil 24 V / 1.2 kΩ = 20 mA; the MOSFET dissipates only I²RDS(on); the flyback diode clamps the turn-off spike.

Common mistakes

Forgetting the inversion

LED on = relay off in this topology; decide which state is fail-safe.

No flyback diode

Hundreds of volts of inductive kick.

24 V straight onto the gate

Destroys a ±20 V gate.

Test and verification

  • Power-cycle the MCU and confirm the relay stays off during reset
  • Scope VDS at turn-off with the diode fitted

Requirements

  • 24 V DC current-sinking input, IEC 61131-2 type 1 or 3 (representative regions: OFF ≤ 5 V; ON 11/15–30 V at 2–15 mA)
  • Reverse-polarity and surge tolerant (IEC 61000-4-5 level per product standard)
  • Low self-heating: 16 inputs per module

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. Threshold Vth ≈ VD1 + VLED·(R1 + R2)/R2 = 0.7 + 1.05 × 4370/470 ≈ 10.5 V.
  2. At 15 V: Iin ≈ (15 − 0.7 − 1.1)/3.9 kΩ ≈ 3.4 mA (≥ 2 mA); ILED ≈ 3.4 − 1.1/470 ≈ 1.0 mA.
  3. At 30 V: Iin ≈ 7.2 mA (≤ 15 mA); PR1 ≈ 0.2 W → 0.5 W part, pulse-rated for surge.
  4. Output: ILED ≈ 1 mA at the ON minimum. With CTREOL ≈ 70 % at 1 mA (rank C × low-current × temperature × ageing), IC ≈ 0.7 mA; a 22 kΩ pull-up needs 0.14 mA → k ≈ 5.

Requirements

  • 12 V / 2 A flyback, current-mode controller with a 5 V-referenced FB pin (RPU internal or external 4.7 kΩ)
  • Crossover ≈ 1–2 kHz; phase margin ≥ 45° over CTR spread and temperature
  • Reinforced isolation mains → SELV (IEC 62368-1)

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. VOUT = 2.495 × (1 + 10k/2.61k) ≈ 12.05 V.
  2. TL431 bias: RBIAS = VF/1 mA ≈ 1.2 kΩ → use 1 kΩ (keeps ≥ 1 mA in the TL431 even when the LED is off).
  3. LED headroom: max LED current = (12 − 1.2 − 2.5 VKA,min)/2.2 kΩ − 1.2 mA (bias) ≈ 2.6 mA. FB pull-down needs ≈ 5 V/4.7 kΩ ≈ 1.1 mA; at CTREOL 50 % that is 2.1 mA of LED current: OK.
  4. Compensator: G = CTR·RPU/RLED = 1.0 × 4.7/2.2 ≈ 2.1 (6.6 dB); mid-band ×(1 + RZ/RUP) = 4.3 (12.6 dB); zero at 1/(2π·10 nF·20 kΩ) ≈ 800 Hz.
  5. With a plant of 6 dB and a 200 Hz pole, crossover ≈ 1.7 kHz and PM ≈ 67° at CTR 100 %. At CTR 300 %, gain rises ≈ 9.5 dB: re-check PM (interactive below).

Common mistakes

Compensating at typical CTR

At max CTR the loop gain is 3× higher: oscillation in some units.

Missing RBIAS

TL431 drops below 1 mA at light load: regulation fails.

Big CFB

Adds a low pole on top of the opto pole: phase margin collapses.

Test and verification

  • Measure the loop with an injection transformer (e.g. 20–50 Ω injection resistor in the divider) at min/max load and line
  • Repeat with a high- and a low-CTR sample, and at hot/cold
  • Load-step test: overshoot and ringing consistent with the measured PM

Requirements

  • Drive a 1200 V, 100 A IGBT module (QG ≈ 600 nC) at 10 kHz from an MCU PWM
  • Reinforced isolation to the control side; DC bus 800 V; switching dV/dt up to ≈ 10 kV/µs (IGBT) or 50+ kV/µs (SiC)
  • Short-circuit protection within ≈ 10 µs

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. LED: IF = (5 − 1.8 − 0.3)/270 Ω ≈ 10.7 mA (within a typical 7–16 mA recommended range).
  2. Peak gate current ≈ (15 − (−8))/(10 + 3 + 2) Ω ≈ 1.5 A ≤ 2.5 A rating.
  3. Gate power = 600 nC × 23 V × 10 kHz ≈ 0.14 W; average supply current = QG·f = 6 mA: size the isolated DC-DC with margin.
  4. CMTI: IGBT at 10 kV/µs → a ≥ 25–35 kV/µs driver gives 2.5×+ margin. For SiC at 50–100 kV/µs choose a driver rated ≥ 100–150 kV/µs.
  5. DESAT: blanking time sized so detection completes within the module’s short-circuit withstand time (≈ 10 µs class).

Test and verification

  • Double-pulse test: VGE, VCE, IC at max bus and current
  • Short-circuit test with DESAT, verify soft turn-off
  • CMTI check: look for glitches on the fault line during switching

Requirements

  • Detect each zero crossing of 230 V / 50 Hz for TRIAC phase control or power metering
  • Timing uncertainty < ±100 µs after calibration; reinforced isolation to the MCU

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. LED peak current ≈ 325 V / 94 kΩ ≈ 3.5 mA; average (rectified) ≈ 2.2 mA.
  2. Each resistor: 115 Vrms → P ≈ 0.28 W; choose 0.5–1 W, voltage-rated ≥ its share of the surge (or 3 in series).
  3. Pulse width: output high while |Vline| < ≈ 30–50 V (depends on CTR, pull-up and threshold) → ≈ 2 × asin(40/325)/(2π·50) ≈ 0.8 ms.
  4. Timing: use the pulse centre (average of rising and falling edges). That cancels the symmetric widening and makes the result nearly independent of CTR.

Requirements

  • UART at 115.2 kbit/s (upgradeable to 1 Mbit/s) between a controller and a field device
  • RS-485 variant: DI, RO, DE isolated, bus side powered by an isolated 5 V supply
  • CMTI ≥ 2× the expected ground-shift dV/dt

Step-by-step calculation (worst case)

Calculation
  1. LED: IF = (3.3 − 0.3 − 1.75)/120 Ω ≈ 10 mA (inside the 6.5–15 mA recommended range).
  2. Pull-up 1 kΩ to 5 V: IOL ≈ 4.4 mA; tr ≈ 2.2 × 1 kΩ × 15 pF ≈ 33 ns.
  3. At 1 Mbit/s: bit = 1000 ns; PWD 35 ns + 33 ns edge ≈ 7 %: comfortable. At 115.2 kbit/s it is < 1 %.
  4. RS-485: DE must enable the driver before the first start bit; the coupler delay (≈ 75 ns) is negligible at these rates.

Worst-case IF, CTREOL, overdrive k ≥ 2, speed, isolation, layout, verification.

MCU I/O

Size RLED with VF,max and driver drop; mind the inversion and fail-safe state.

PLC inputs

A shunt resistor sets a clean threshold; watch resistor power and surge.

Feedback loops

CTR spread changes loop gain: design PM at CTRmax, regulation at CTRmin.

Gate drives

Bipolar supply, split RG, local bypass, CMTI margin, DESAT.

Zero-cross

Resistors carry mains power; time from the pulse centre.

Serial links

PWD + edge ≤ 10–20 % of a bit; bypass and pull-up per datasheet.

Verify at the corners

Hot, cold, low-CTR samples, 50 % IF as an EOL simulation.

Part D — Engineering Scenarios

Scenario 1 — Relay chatters at power-up
In Example 2 the relay briefly energises every time the MCU resets. Why, and how do you fix it?
Analysis. During reset the GPIO is high-impedance: the LED is off, the phototransistor is off, and the 10 kΩ pull-up turns the MOSFET on. The topology makes “LED off” = relay on. Fix: invert the output stage (phototransistor drives the MOSFET gate high through a pull-down arrangement) so LED off = relay off, or add a pull-down on the MCU side that keeps the LED on during reset (less clean). Choose the fail-safe state deliberately.
Scenario 2 — Input that turns on with nothing connected
A PLC input without R2 (no LED shunt) reads ON when connected to a 2-wire proximity sensor that leaks 1.5 mA in its off state. Explain using Example 3.
Analysis. Without the shunt, 1.5 mA flows entirely through the LED. At CTR ≈ 100 % that is 1.5 mA of collector current, easily enough to pull a 22 kΩ pull-up low. The standard allows off-state currents up to 1.5 mA (type 3). Fix: add R2 so that off-state current flows through the shunt (1.5 mA × 470 Ω ≈ 0.7 V < VF): the LED stays dark until the input exceeds the threshold.
Scenario 3 — Oscillation with a new CTR rank
A flyback designed with rank A (80–160 %) is changed to rank D (300–600 %) for supply reasons. Some units now oscillate. Explain and fix.
Analysis. Loop gain scales with CTR: rank D can be up to 600/160 ≈ 3.75× (11.5 dB) higher, pushing the crossover up towards the opto pole and reducing phase margin. Fix: scale RPU/RLED down by ≈ the CTR ratio, re-tune RZ/CZ, and re-measure the loop at both CTR extremes. Lock the rank in the BOM.
