Why Thermal Management Matters

55% of all electronic failures are thermally induced. For every 10°C increase in junction temperature, component lifetime halves (Arrhenius relationship). A properly designed heatsink is the difference between a reliable product and a field failure.

The Problem

A 25W FPGA in still air reaches 150°C junction temperature within seconds. Maximum rated TJ is 100°C. Without thermal management, the device fails immediately.

The Solution

A properly sized heatsink reduces thermal resistance from junction to ambient, maintaining safe operating temperatures with documented margins under worst-case conditions.

The Outcome

Junction temperature stays within specification. Product achieves rated lifetime. Thermal margin documented for manufacturing variation. No field returns.

The Thermal Resistance Network

Heat flows from the silicon junction through a chain of thermal resistances to reach ambient air. Each interface adds resistance. Understanding this chain is the foundation of every thermal design.

⚡
Key Equation: TJ = TA + P × (RθJC + RθCS + RθSA). This single equation drives every heatsink design decision.
RθJC — Junction-to-Case resistance. Fixed by the IC package. Typically 0.5–5 °C/W for power devices.
RθCS — Case-to-Sink resistance. Controlled by TIM selection. Range: 0.05–1.0 °C/W depending on material.
RθSA — Sink-to-Ambient resistance. The heatsink's job. This is what you design.

Heat Spreading & Fin Efficiency

A concentrated heat source must spread through the heatsink base before reaching the fins. The base thickness and material conductivity determine how effectively heat distributes. Fins far from the source contribute less — this is fin efficiency.

Natural vs. Forced Convection

Natural convection relies on buoyancy-driven airflow (h = 5–25 W/m²K). Forced convection uses fans to dramatically increase the heat transfer coefficient (h = 25–250 W/m²K). The choice defines your heatsink size by an order of magnitude.
