Coupling Paths & Victim Circuits

RF couples in through cables acting as antennas and gets demodulated at nonlinear junctions. Learn to find the victim and the entry path.

Module progress0%

The dominant RF entry path is the cable: at the test frequencies a cable is an efficient antenna, so it collects RF and delivers common-mode current into the product. That current flows through the board's ground system and develops voltages that reach victim circuits — exactly the reciprocal of how cables radiate in the emissions course.

Learning objectives. Identify cable pickup and demodulation; and recognize high-impedance victim nodes.

RF itself is often out of band, but any nonlinearity — a diode, an ESD-protection cell, a transistor base/gate, an op-amp input — rectifies the modulated carrier, producing a DC shift plus the 1 kHz envelope right in the victim's operating band. That is why an 80% AM signal at 300 MHz can appear as noise on a slow sensor line.

Prime victims. High-impedance/high-gain analog inputs, references, reset lines, and feedback nodes — small induced voltages matter most there.
Not the digital core. Robust digital logic is usually resilient; the failure is almost always at an analog or control interface.

Susceptibility is frequency-selective, so note which frequencies fail — that hints at a resonant cable length or a specific victim. On the bench, inject with a hand probe/antenna while monitoring the suspect signal to localize the entry point and the vulnerable band, using the delta discipline from the Pre-Compliance course.
