Overview

What Are Stitching Vias?

Stitching vias are ground vias placed at regular intervals to electrically connect multiple ground planes, copper pours, or ground stitching around high-speed traces. When correctly placed, they reduce slot antenna effects, lower return-path inductance, and improve shielding by preventing E-field leakage between layers.

Common misconception: Engineers often add stitching vias generously — believing more is always better. In practice, via spacing that exceeds λ/20 at the target frequency turns each via gap into a slot antenna, amplifying rather than suppressing radiation.
Engineering note: A row of vias spaced > λ/2 apart is electromagnetically transparent — it provides zero shielding at that frequency.

01

Via Gap → Slot Antenna

The gap between two vias radiates like a half-wave dipole

What happens

When two stitching vias are separated by more than λ/2 at a given frequency, the slot between them resonates as a half-wavelength slot antenna. Current flowing along the plane edge excites this slot, which radiates efficiently.

Formula

fres = c / (2 × d × √εr)

d = via spacing, εr = substrate permittivity

Example

Via spacing = 30 mm, FR4 (εr = 4.3):
fres = 3×10⁸ / (2 × 0.03 × 2.07) ≈ 2.4 GHz
→ Peak radiation exactly at Wi-Fi band!

Why it matters

  • Shielding goes from +20 dB to −10 dB at resonance
  • Net effect: vias make EMI worse at that frequency
  • Often discovered only during pre-compliance testing

Correct solution

  • Limit spacing to d < λ/20 (not λ/2)
  • At 1 GHz in FR4: d_max ≈ 7.2 mm
  • At 5 GHz in FR4: d_max ≈ 1.4 mm
02

Via Inductance → LC Resonance

Each via is an inductor — it resonates with plane capacitance

Via inductance model

Lvia ≈ 0.2h · [ln(4h/d) − 1] nH

h = via height (mm), d = via diameter (mm)

Example

h = 1.6 mm, d = 0.3 mm:
L = 0.2 × 1.6 × [ln(21.3) − 1] ≈ 0.80 nH

With 10 pF inter-plane capacitance:
fres = 1/(2π√LC) ≈ 1.78 GHz

Failure signatures

  • Impedance spike on PDN at resonance frequency
  • Ground bounce increased, not reduced
  • Shielding effectiveness dips at via resonance
  • Multiple vias create multiple LC modes

Mitigation

  • Use larger via diameter (reduces L)
  • Minimise via stub length
  • Back-drill stubs on high-speed designs
  • Add SMT bypass caps near vias
03

Periodic Structure Passband

Regular via arrays create waveguide-like passbands

Electromagnetic bandgap (EBG) effect

A uniform array of vias forms a periodic structure that has frequency-dependent transmission characteristics — alternating stopbands and passbands, exactly like a waveguide filter or photonic crystal.

Stopband frequency

fstop = c / (2s√εeff)

s = period spacing, εeff = effective permittivity

Key insight

Between stopbands, the structure has a passband where noise propagates freely. An engineer who adds stitching vias to suppress 1 GHz noise may inadvertently create a 2 GHz passband that was previously blocked.

Design approach

  • Use irregular via spacing to randomise resonance modes
  • Target stopband to contain the dominant noise harmonic
  • Simulate with a full-wave EM solver (HFSS, CST)
04

Cavity Resonance in Power Plane

Via rings around power islands excite plane cavity modes

Parallel plate cavity

A power/ground plane pair forms a parallel-plate transmission line cavity. The lowest resonance mode appears when the plane dimension equals λ/2 (TM10 mode).

fmn = (c/2√εr) · √[(m/L)² + (n/W)²]

L, W = plane dimensions, m, n = mode indices

Via stitching around island

A via ring that isn't dense enough excites the island as a resonant patch antenna — similar to a microstrip antenna deliberately designed for maximum radiation.

Real-world failure

  • 3.3 V island (50 × 30 mm) resonates at ~2.0 GHz
  • SMPS switching harmonics excite the cavity
  • Via ring at 8 mm pitch: 5th harmonic (500 MHz × 4) hits passband
  • Result: 15 dB increase in radiated emissions at 2 GHz

Solution

  • Keep via pitch < λ/20 at highest relevant harmonic
  • Partition power islands using ferrite beads, not just vias
  • Add distributed bypass capacitors to damp cavity Q

Industry Application Examples

Automotive

CAN FD — 5 Mbit/s

Max via spacing ≈ 14 mm. Common failure: 30 mm spacing resonates at 2.3 GHz, coupling into GNSS antenna trace on same board. ISO 11452-5 failure.

Consumer

Wi-Fi 6E — 6 GHz

Maximum via spacing = 2.4 mm. Achievable only with laser-drilled microvias. Via fence around RF IC is mandatory — not optional.

Industrial

RS-485 — 10 Mbit/s

5th harmonic at 25 MHz. Via spacing < 60 mm sufficient. Common error: adding stitching vias around 48 V buck converter — 2 MHz switching × 10th harmonic = 20 MHz — resonates with 75 mm via gaps.

Medical

USB 2.0 + RF coexistence

USB 2.0 harmonics at 480 MHz fundamental. Via spacing < 7.2 mm required. IEC 60601-1-2 requires 6 dB immunity margin — poorly stitched boards fail in the first pre-scan.

Common Mistakes

The Five Most Frequent Via Stitching Errors

M1

Spacing determined by aesthetics

Symptom: Board looks well stitched, fails EMC at 2+ GHz

Engineers often route vias on a 10 mm grid matching the PCB silkscreen grid — not based on the actual highest frequency harmonic. At 5 GHz, 10 mm is 7× too large.

Fix: Calculate d_max before placement. Use DRC rule in your EDA tool.
M2

Stitching around non-problematic areas

Symptom: Extra vias increase cost, no EMC improvement

Adding stitching vias along low-frequency power traces or I/O lines that carry no high-frequency content wastes copper and drill hits but does not prevent failures at the actual noise source.

Fix: Identify noise sources first. Place vias where return current flows.
M3

Stitching across split planes

Symptom: Ground stitch bridges isolated power domains

Connecting a digital ground pour to an analog reference via stitching vias defeats the entire purpose of plane splitting. Noise couples directly through the vias.

Fix: Never stitch across isolation barriers. Use only at board edge or star-point.
M4

Forgetting via stubs

Symptom: Differential channel passes at 5 Gbps, fails at 10 Gbps

Through-hole vias that only connect two internal layers have a stub extending to the opposite side. This stub resonates at f = c/(4×stub_length×√εr) and creates a deep null in the insertion loss.

Fix: Back-drill stubs to <10 mil remaining. Use blind vias where possible.
M5

Uneven ground pour connection

Symptom: One corner of board radiates 8 dB more than others

A ground pour connected to the main ground plane by only 1–2 vias has high inductance at the connection point. RF current flows around the high-inductance path, creating uneven field distribution.

Fix: Minimum 4 stitching vias per 1 cm² of isolated pour area.

⊘ No stitching vias needed here.

This area does not benefit from stitching. Adding vias would increase cost and drill count with no EMC benefit.

✓ Add a few vias for return-path continuity.

Use 4–8 mm pitch is not critical here. Aim for one via per 15–20 mm of ground pour edge. Verify no DC isolation bridges are crossed.

✓ Add stitching vias at ≤ 14 mm pitch.

Use 0.3 mm drill, 0.6 mm pad. Place in two rows flanking any high-speed trace within 3× trace width. Verify no plane splits are crossed.

✓ Add dense stitching at ≤ 7 mm pitch.

Via fence within 2 mm of signal trace. Two rows. Connect top, L2-GND, bottom-GND. Verify with post-layout EM extraction in HyperLynx or similar.

⚠ Microvias required — verify with EM simulation.

Standard PTH vias at < 4 mm pitch are cost-prohibitive. Use HDI microvias or pad-to-pad stitching. Simulate in HFSS/CST before fabrication. Consider co-planar waveguide instead of microstrip to eliminate the need for return-path vias.

Knowledge Check

Engineering Calculation Exercise

Given:

  • PCB substrate: FR4, εr = 4.5
  • Highest-speed signal: USB 3.2 Gen 2 — 10 Gbps, 5 GHz fundamental
  • Available via drill: 0.2 mm (laser via), via height 0.4 mm (2-layer HDI)

Calculate:

  1. Maximum via spacing for λ/20 shielding at 5 GHz
  2. Via inductance for the 0.2 mm drill, 0.4 mm height via
  3. Self-resonance of that via with 2 pF inter-layer capacitance

Solution

1. Maximum via spacing:
d_max = c / (20 × f × √εr)
d_max = 3×10⁸ / (20 × 5×10⁹ × √4.5)
d_max = 3×10⁸ / (10¹¹ × 2.121)
d_max ≈ 1.41 mm — requires HDI microvias
2. Via inductance:
L = 0.2 × 0.4 × [ln(4×0.4/0.2) − 1]
L = 0.08 × [ln(8) − 1]
L = 0.08 × [2.079 − 1]
L ≈ 0.086 nH
3. Self-resonance with 2 pF:
fres = 1 / (2π × √(0.086×10⁻⁹ × 2×10⁻¹²))
fres = 1 / (2π × √(1.72×10⁻²²))
fres = 1 / (2π × 1.31×10⁻¹¹)
fres ≈ 12.1 GHz — comfortably above 5 GHz ✓
Key takeaway: At 5 GHz, you need 1.4 mm via spacing — physically impossible with standard PTH vias (min drill ~0.2–0.25 mm with 0.5 mm pads). HDI with stacked microvias is mandatory.
