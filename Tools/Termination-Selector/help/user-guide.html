1. Introduction

Purpose

The Termination Selector is a browser-based interactive learning tool that teaches signal integrity termination — series, parallel, Thevenin, AC (RC), and differential — through live calculation, an animated bounce-diagram simulation, real engineering examples, and a scored quiz. It is part of the Rising Edge Technologies engineering tool suite.

Intended users

Hardware and PCB design engineers with roughly 5–7 years of experience will find the calculator and decision tree immediately useful for day-to-day design decisions. The concept explorer, animations, and quiz are written to also be approachable for engineers new to signal integrity.

What the tool does

It computes every termination scheme's resistor/capacitor values from your line and driver parameters, recommends the most appropriate scheme for your topology and power budget, simulates the resulting receiver waveform with an educational bounce-diagram model, and reinforces the material with animated diagrams, worked examples, a decision tree, and a quiz.

What the tool does not do

The live simulation is a simplified lattice-diagram approximation for teaching the reflection mechanism — it is not a substitute for a full SPICE or 3D field-solver simulation when validating a real board design. Always confirm final resistor values against your actual field-solved stack-up impedance and verify with TDR measurement on hardware.

Version history: Version 1.0 is the initial release, covering series, parallel, Thevenin, AC and differential termination with a live calculator, bounce-diagram simulation, three Plotly charts, two animated SVGs, an interactive decision tree, a 10 question quiz, and a 27-section engineering notes accordion.

2. Quick Start Tutorial

  1. Read the Concept Explorer. Six tabs at the top of the page cover each termination type plus a quick decision-guide table.
  2. Watch the animated diagrams. See a wave ring at an open end versus being absorbed at a terminated end, and compare the current paths of series/parallel/Thevenin networks.
  3. Enter your parameters into the calculator. Z0, VDD, driver impedance, driver type, topology, bus standard, bit rate, VTT availability, and power budget priority — the recommendation and every formula update live.
  4. Review the charts. Reflection coefficient curve, receiver waveform comparison, and power dissipation comparison, all zoomable and exportable to PNG.
  5. Drag the simulation sliders. See the bounce-diagram waveform respond instantly to impedance, termination resistance, and trace length.
  6. Read the examples, design review, mistakes, and myths sections to connect the theory to real designs.
  7. Walk the decision tree for a scheme recommendation with rationale for your specific net.
  8. Take the quiz to check your understanding — ten scored questions with explanations.
  9. Export your notes or configuration from the Reports & Export card, or print the whole lesson.
Tip: Click Play Lesson in the hero to have the floating narrator widget read a short guided script aloud while auto-scrolling through the key sections.

5. Animated Diagrams

Reflection: open end vs. terminated end

An animated wavefront travels from the driver to the far end. On the open/unterminated row it doubles and bounces back and forth (ringing), decaying over successive round trips. On the terminated row it is absorbed on first arrival — a single clean transition.

Current path: series vs. parallel vs. Thevenin

Three schematic diagrams show which resistors conduct continuously (parallel, Thevenin — animated dashed current flow) versus which only conduct momentarily during switching (series — a periodic pulse). Hover any element for a tooltip explaining its role.

Both diagrams have a Replay button that restarts the animation from the beginning. Animations respect prefers-reduced-motion and are disabled for users who have that OS-level preference set.

6. Termination Calculator

The calculator computes all five termination schemes simultaneously from a shared set of inputs, then highlights the recommended scheme with a colored badge and a plain-language reason.

Recommendation engine logic (summary)

  1. Differential bus standard → always recommend differential termination.
  2. Multi-drop topology or RS-485 bus standard → parallel-to-VTT if a VTT rail is available, otherwise Thevenin.
  3. Clock distribution with a tight power budget → AC (RC) termination.
  4. Daisy-chain topology → series termination at each stage.
  5. Open-drain driver → parallel termination (the pull-up doubles as termination).
  6. Default (point-to-point, push-pull driver) → series termination.

The exact logic lives in recommendScheme() in js/calculations.js.

8. Live Reflection Simulation

Three sliders (Z0, termination resistance, trace length) and a termination-type selector (None / Series / Parallel) drive a bounce-diagram calculation in js/simulations.js, which is redrawn on every slider change.

How the model works

A step is launched from the source with amplitude VDD × Z0/(Z0+Rsource). It travels one propagation delay to the load, where it is scaled by the load reflection coefficient ΓL and reflected. The reflected wave travels back to the source, scaled by ΓS, and so on for several round trips. The cumulative voltage at the receiver is plotted as a stair-step waveform.

This is a lumped-reflection educational approximation using a fixed propagation velocity (~1.5×108 m/s, typical FR-4 stripline) — real designs should be validated with a field solver and TDR measurement.

11. Common Mistakes & Engineering Myths

Six structured mistake cards (root cause / symptoms / impact / correct approach) cover mismatched resistor values, misplaced termination, power-constrained parallel termination, AC-coupling interaction, differential resistor tolerance, and ignoring driver output impedance.

Four myth cards (why it exists / where it fails / correct practice) address "always terminate everything," "series termination replaces controlled impedance," "parallel is always better," and "resistor precision doesn't matter."

12. Interactive Decision Tree

A click-through question tree starting with "is this a differential pair?" and branching through topology, driver type, and V_TT rail availability to a recommended scheme with a short rationale. A breadcrumb trail shows your path, and Restart resets to the first question. The tree logic lives in the TREE and RESULTS objects in js/main.js.

13. Quiz

Ten multiple-choice questions in js/quiz.js cover the reflection coefficient formula, series/parallel/Thevenin/AC derivations, differential impedance, topology suitability, RS-485 termination rules, and TDR verification. Each answer locks in immediately with an explanation. A final score screen shows a percentage and a qualitative tier.

18. Engineering Calculations Reference

All calculations are implemented as pure functions in js/calculations.js, exported on window.TSCalc. 

Γ = (Z_L − Z0) / (Z_L + Z0)

Series:    R_S = Z0 − R_driver
           V_launch = V_DD × Z0 / (Z0 + R_S + R_driver)

Parallel:  R_P = Z0
           P = (V_DD − V_ref)² / R_P

Thevenin:  R1‖R2 = Z0  and  R2/(R1+R2) × V_DD = V_bias
           R2 = Z0 × V_DD / V_bias
           R1 = Z0 × V_DD / (V_DD − V_bias)
           P = V_DD² / (R1 + R2)

AC (RC):   R = Z0
           τ = R × C ≈ 6 × T_bit  =>  C = τ / R

Differential: Z_diff ≈ 2 × Z0 × coupling_factor

The live bounce-diagram simulation lives in js/simulations.js (bounceDiagram()), and the Plotly chart builders live in js/charts.js.

20. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the calculator show all five schemes instead of just the recommended one?

Comparing all five side by side is often more useful than a single answer — you can see the tradeoffs (power, complexity, effectiveness) directly rather than trusting a black box.

Is the live simulation accurate enough for a real design?

It is intentionally simplified to make the reflection mechanism visible. Use it to build intuition, then validate the final design with a field solver and TDR measurement on hardware.

Why does my differential impedance not exactly equal 2× the single-ended Z0?

Coupling between the two traces of a differential pair lowers the odd-mode impedance below the isolated single-ended value — the calculator applies a coupling factor to approximate this.

Can I save my calculator inputs and come back later?

Yes — use Save config in the Reports & Export card, then Load config on a future visit.

21. Troubleshooting

Charts are not rendering

Charts require Plotly.js from cdn.plot.ly — verify your network/firewall allows the CDN. The calculator and decision tree work without it.

The narrator doesn't speak

Some browsers (or OS configurations) do not support the SpeechSynthesis API, or have no voices installed. The narrator widget still displays the script text so the lesson remains usable.

The tour doesn't highlight the expected section

Ensure you have not manually removed or renamed the section IDs (#tour-*) — the tour looks up each step by CSS selector and skips to the next step (or ends) if a target is missing.

22. Glossary

Z0
Characteristic impedance of a transmission line.
Γ
Reflection coefficient, (Z_L − Z0)/(Z_L + Z0).
V_TT
A termination voltage rail, often V_DD/2.
ODT
On-Die Termination.
Z_diff
Differential impedance of a coupled pair.
Bounce/lattice diagram
Graphical method for tracking successive reflections over time and distance.
TDR
Time-Domain Reflectometry.
