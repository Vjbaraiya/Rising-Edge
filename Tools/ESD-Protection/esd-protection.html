PCB Layout Guidelines

Critical placement and routing requirements for effective ESD protection.

📍

Place TVS at Connector Edge

TVS diodes must be the first component the ESD pulse encounters — within 5 mm of the connector pins.

  • Route ESD current path before any trace branches
  • Minimize inductance between connector pin and TVS anode
  • Trace length from connector to TVS: < 5 mm
⏚

Ground Return Path

The TVS ground connection is as critical as the signal connection. A poor ground path negates protection entirely.

  • Multiple vias to ground plane directly under TVS
  • Ground via pitch ≤ 0.5 mm for low inductance
  • Avoid routing ground through thin traces — use planes
→

Signal Routing Order

PCB trace must flow: Connector → TVS → Protected IC. Never branch before the TVS or the pulse bypasses protection.

  • Connector → short trace → TVS → trace → IC
  • No stubs or T-branches before the TVS element
  • For differential pairs, maintain symmetry through TVS
⚡

Parasitic Capacitance Budget

TVS capacitance directly impacts signal integrity. Match CLINE to your data rate requirements.

  • USB 2.0 HS (480 Mbps): CLINE < 1.0 pF
  • USB 3.0 SS (5 Gbps): CLINE < 0.2 pF
  • HDMI 2.0 (6 Gbps): CLINE < 0.15 pF
  • Ethernet 10/100: CLINE < 5 pF acceptable
⚠

Clamping Voltage Verification

VC at the peak ESD pulse current must be below the absolute maximum voltage of the protected IC pin.

  • VC < VABS MAX of IC (with margin)
  • Check VC at actual ESD current, not at 1 mA
  • Account for trace inductance adding to VC
  • Use TLP (Transmission Line Pulse) data when available
🛡

Multi-Stage Protection

For harsh environments (IEC 61000-4-5 surge), use a two-stage approach combining a GDT/MOV with a fast TVS.

  • Stage 1 (primary): GDT or MOV absorbs bulk energy
  • Decoupling inductor or resistor between stages
  • Stage 2 (secondary): Fast TVS clamps residual
  • Required for Ethernet, RS-485, outdoor feeds

What is ESD?

Electrostatic discharge is the rapid, spontaneous transfer of charge between two objects at different potentials. A human body walking on carpet can accumulate 10–35 kV. When a charged person touches an IC pin, the stored charge equalises in under 1 nanosecond.

Why ICs fail: Modern CMOS gate oxides are 2–5 nm thick and rupture at as little as 2–3 V across the dielectric. A 15 kV discharge that reaches the gate is fatal — even if the current is only microamperes.

Human Body Model (HBM)

The HBM circuit models the human body as a 100 pF capacitor in series with a 1500 Ω resistor. It produces a discharge with a 2–10 ns rise time and a ~150 ns decay.

E = ½ C V²  |  I_pk = V / R

At 8 kV HBM: E ≈ 3.2 mJ, I_pk ≈ 5.3 A. That is enough energy to permanently destroy an unprotected CMOS input.

Inside a TVS Diode

A transient voltage suppressor is a heavily doped PN junction engineered for large avalanche current capacity. Unlike a signal Zener (which targets precision voltage reference), a TVS is optimised for peak pulse power — 200 W to 30 kW for 1 ms.

Key structural features

  • Thick epilayer — wide depletion region absorbs more energy
  • Guard rings — prevent avalanche at die edge (field crowding)
  • Low-ohmic substrate — minimises Rdyn → lower VC
  • Large junction area — distributes current density, prevents hot spots
Unidirectional vs Bidirectional: A unidirectional TVS has one PN junction in reverse (high VBR) and a forward diode drop (~0.7 V) in the other direction. A bidirectional (symmetrical) device stacks two junctions back-to-back, giving equal clamping in both polarities.
V_C = V_BR + I_PP × R_dyn
R_dyn ≈ (V_C − V_BR) / I_PP

Clamping Voltage vs Breakdown Voltage

Engineers often confuse VBR with VC. They are different parameters that matter at different operating points.

Parameter Typical value Meaning
VRWM 5.0 V Max DC working voltage (no conduction)
VBR 6.0–6.6 V Breakdown at 1 mA test current
VC 10.0 V @ 16 A Clamping at IEC L4 peak current
IPP 15–30 A Peak pulse current (8/20 µs)
Rdyn 0.25 Ω Drives VC above VBR
Design rule: VC at peak IEC current must be below the IC's absolute maximum input voltage. VBR alone is not a safe indicator — at 30 A, Rdyn can add 4–8 V.

Capacitance and Signal Integrity

Every TVS diode adds capacitance to the signal line. At high data rates, this creates a low-pass filter that closes the eye diagram and increases bit-error rate.

f-3dB = 1 / (2π × Z0 × CLINE)

For a 50 Ω system with CLINE = 1 pF: f-3dB ≈ 3.2 GHz

Unidirectional vs Bidirectional TVS

Choosing the wrong polarity configuration is a common design mistake that leads to either inadequate protection or unwanted signal clamping.

Property Unidirectional Bidirectional
Negative clamping ~0.7 V (forward diode) Symmetric VBR
Positive clamping VC (high) VC (same)
Leakage at VRWM Lower Higher
AC / differential use Not suitable Required
Polarity error risk Reversed = no protection None
Use unidirectional on single-ended DC-biased signals (GPIO, power rails). Use bidirectional on AC-coupled signals, differential pairs, and any line that swings both ways (USB D+/D−, RS-485, audio).

EOS vs ESD — What's the Difference?

Electrical Overstress (EOS) and Electrostatic Discharge are both transient failure modes, but they differ fundamentally in duration, energy, and cause.

Property ESD EOS
Duration < 1 µs 1 µs – minutes
Energy < 1 mJ mJ – kJ
Cause Charged object / body Power fault, inductive kick, supply transient
Failure mode Gate oxide rupture, junction punch-through Metal trace melt, bond wire fuse, oxide degrade
TVS helps? Yes — primary protection Partial — need TVS + transient suppressor
Misdiagnosis is common: Many "ESD failures" in the field are actually EOS events caused by power supply noise, motor back-EMF, or cable inductive kickback. TVS alone won't fix these — add a bulk capacitor + ferrite bead or a TVS with higher energy rating (PPPK ≥ 600 W).

IEC 61000-4-2 Test Waveform

This is the international ESD immunity standard for end-equipment (not IC-level). The contact discharge test is the most aggressive — a charged electrode touches the EUT directly.

Test levels

30 A
±8 kV
Waveform key specs: Rise time ≤ 0.8 ns (10%→90%) · First peak at 0.8–1 ns · Decay to 50% at ~30 ns · Second hump (discharge network) peaks ~10–20 ns.

TVS selection: the device must absorb the waveform energy and clamp below the IC's absolute max in < 1 ns. "Fast" Zeners with slow avalanche response will fail.

PCB Layout Best Practices

Even a correctly chosen TVS fails to protect if the PCB layout is wrong. The goal: the ESD current must flow through the TVS to GND before reaching the IC pin, with the shortest possible path.

The 6 rules

  1. Place TVS within 3 mm of the connector pin
  2. Route: Connector → TVS → IC (never the reverse)
  3. TVS GND via must connect to chassis/PCB GND plane directly — no traces
  4. Avoid 90° trace bends near TVS — they create inductance
  5. Never share TVS GND via with decoupling cap vias (limits peak current)
  6. Pour GND copper around TVS footprint to reduce thermal resistance
A 10 mm trace adds ~10 nH of inductance. At 30 A / 1 ns, that generates VL = L × dI/dt = 300 V — which defeats the TVS completely.

Common ESD Design Mistakes

CRITICAL

TVS placed after the IC

ESD current flows through the IC before reaching the TVS. Correct: connector → TVS → IC.

Move TVS within 3 mm of connector, upstream of IC.
CRITICAL

Wrong polarity TVS

Unidirectional TVS installed backward — forward diode faces the rail instead of GND. Provides zero protection.

Verify anode → signal, cathode → GND for unidirectional. Use bidirectional for AC/differential.
HIGH

C_LINE exceeds SI budget

Using a 2 pF TVS on a USB 3.0 line (budget: 0.2 pF) collapses the eye diagram at 5 Gbps.

Select process-optimised device (GaN/SiGe). Measure CLINE at frequency, not just datasheet Cj0.
HIGH

V_C exceeds IC abs max

Selecting TVS by V_BR only — forgetting R_dyn adds 4–8 V at 30 A peak. IC gets 18 V spike instead of 10 V.

Look up V_C at IEC peak current (I_PP column), not at 1 mA test current.
HIGH

Long GND return path

TVS GND routed through a shared trace — 10 mm of trace adds ~10 nH, injecting 300 V at 30 A / 1 ns.

Dedicated via directly to GND plane. No shared traces with other components.
MEDIUM

Missing secondary stage

Single TVS on a long cable run — primary surge saturates TVS and residual energy still damages IC.

Two-stage: GDT or MOV at cable entry + TVS near IC, with series resistor/bead between stages.
MEDIUM

Using ESD rating as EOS protection

TVS rated for ESD (µJ energy) installed on a 24 V power line with inductive loads — TVS immediately fails on first motor shutoff.

Use TVS with P_PPK ≥ 600 W (8/20 µs pulse), or TRANSZORB / MOV for inductive load energy.
MEDIUM

Differential pairs: protecting only one leg

Single TVS on USB D+ only. D− is unprotected and the asymmetric load degrades CMRR.

Use matched dual TVS array (e.g., PRTR5V0U2X) that protects D+ and D− simultaneously.
