1. Introduction

Purpose

The Transmission Line Calculator is a browser-based interactive learning tool that teaches PCB transmission-line characteristic impedance — microstrip, embedded microstrip, stripline, asymmetric stripline, differential microstrip, differential stripline, and grounded coplanar waveguide (CPWG) — through live calculation, animated cross-section diagrams, real engineering examples, and a scored quiz. It is part of the Rising Edge Technologies engineering tool suite.

Intended users

Hardware and PCB design engineers with roughly 5–7 years of experience will find the calculator and decision tree immediately useful for day-to-day stack-up and routing decisions. The concept explorer, animations, and quiz are written to also be approachable for engineers new to signal integrity.

What the tool does

It computes Z0 (or Zdiff for differential geometries), effective dielectric constant εeff, and propagation delay from your trace geometry using closed-form Hammerstad-Jensen / IPC-2141-style formulas, shows a proportional cross-section diagram that updates live, and reinforces the material with animated diagrams, worked examples, a decision tree, and a quiz.

What the tool does not do

The closed-form formulas are first-order engineering approximations, not a full 2D/3D field solution — they are accurate enough for early stack-up planning and design review, but always confirm final trace geometry against your fab house's field-solver / impedance-control service before release, and verify fabricated boards with TDR measurement.

Version history: Version 1.0 is the initial release, covering seven geometries with a live calculator, geometry-aware cross-section visualization, a slider-driven live simulation, three Plotly charts, two animated SVGs, an interactive decision tree, a 13-question quiz, and a 31-section engineering notes accordion.

2. Quick Start Tutorial

  1. Read the Concept Explorer. Seven tabs at the top of the page cover each transmission-line geometry.
  2. Watch the animated diagrams. Compare microstrip vs. stripline field distribution, and see how differential pair coupling changes with spacing.
  3. Pick a geometry and enter your dimensions into the calculator. W, T, H (or B / H1 / H2 depending on geometry), S or G where relevant, and εr — Z0, εeff, and propagation delay update live, and the cross-section diagram resizes to match.
  4. Review the charts. Z0 vs. trace width, Z0 vs. dielectric height, and Zdiff vs. pair spacing, all zoomable and exportable to PNG.
  5. Drag the simulation sliders. See the impedance curve and cross-section respond instantly to width, height, and dielectric constant.
  6. Read the examples, design review, mistakes, and myths sections to connect the theory to real designs.
  7. Walk the decision tree for a recommended geometry with rationale for your specific net.
  8. Take the quiz to check your understanding — thirteen scored questions with explanations.
  9. Export your notes or configuration from the Reports & Export card, or print the whole lesson.
Tip: Click Play Lesson in the hero to have the floating narrator widget read a short guided script aloud while auto-scrolling through the key sections.

4. Concept Explorer

Seven tabs, each with a small schematic figure and a bulleted fact list:

Tab Core idea
Microstrip Trace over one plane, field partly in air — simplest, fastest, least shielded.
Embedded Microstrip Same as microstrip but covered by dielectric — εeff rises, Z0 drops slightly.
Stripline Trace centered between two planes, εeff = εr, fully shielded.
Asymmetric Stripline Trace offset between two unequal plane spacings H1, H2.
Differential Microstrip Coupled pair over one plane; Zdiff depends on spacing S vs. H.

5. Animated Diagrams

Microstrip vs. stripline field distribution

Two cross-section panels show field lines around a microstrip trace (partly in air, partly in dielectric, one reference plane) versus a stripline trace (fully enclosed between two planes). The field-line paths pulse gently to draw attention to the difference in enclosure.

Differential pair coupling vs. spacing

A differential-pair cross-section with three selectable spacing states (tight, medium, loose) shows the coupling field between the two traces strengthening as spacing decreases. Hover any element for a tooltip explaining its role.

Both diagrams have a Replay button that restarts the animation from the beginning. Animations respect prefers-reduced-motion and are disabled for users who have that OS-level preference set.

6. Z0 / Zdiff Calculator

Select a geometry from the dropdown — the form automatically shows only the inputs relevant to that geometry (for example, stripline shows total plane spacing B instead of dielectric height H). Every remaining field recalculates Z0/Zdiff, εeff, propagation delay, and an estimated fabrication tolerance band live as you type.

Formula summary

Geometry Formula family
Microstrip Hammerstad εeff + IPC-2141 closed-form Z0
Embedded microstrip Surface microstrip Z0 scaled by √(εeff,surface/εeff,embedded)
Stripline (symmetric) IPC-2141 closed-form Z0, εeff = εr
Stripline (asymmetric) Harmonic-mean effective spacing + symmetric-stripline formula
Differential microstrip Zdiff = 2·Z0·[1 − 0.48·exp(−0.96·S/H)]
Differential stripline Zdiff = 2·Z0·[1 − 0.347·exp(−2.9·S/B)]
CPWG Conformal-mapping CPW (elliptic integral ratio) combined in parallel with a microstrip bottom-plane term

The exact implementation lives in js/calculations.js.

8. Live Geometry Simulation

Three sliders (trace width, dielectric height, dielectric constant) drive a microstrip Z0 calculation in js/simulations.js, redrawn on every slider change. The accompanying chart sweeps width at the current height/εr, marking your current slider position, while a separate cross-section diagram resizes proportionally so you can see the physical picture change alongside the number.

The live simulation always uses the microstrip formula for clarity and a fixed 0.035 mm (1 oz) copper thickness. Use the full calculator (section 6) for the other six geometries.

11. Common Mistakes & Engineering Myths

Six structured mistake cards (root cause / symptoms / impact / correct approach) cover using εr instead of εeff, ignoring trace thickness/etch factor, ignoring the soldermask effect on microstrip, assuming the wrong reference plane, ignoring adjacent-trace coupling, and forgetting manufacturing tolerance.

Four myth cards (why it exists / where it fails / correct practice) address "50 Ω is always the target," "stripline is always better than microstrip," "impedance only depends on trace width," and "a closed-form calculator is exact."

12. Interactive Decision Tree

A click-through question tree starting with "is this signal differential?" and branching through RF-vs-digital, EMI sensitivity, and stack-up layer budget to a recommended geometry with a short rationale. A breadcrumb trail shows your path, and Restart resets to the first question. The tree logic lives in the TREE and RESULTS objects in js/main.js.

18. Engineering Calculations Reference

All calculations are implemented as pure functions in js/calculations.js, exported on window.TLCCalc. 

Microstrip:
  εeff = (εr+1)/2 + (εr−1)/2 × 1/√(1+12H/W)
  Z0   = (87/√(εr+1.41)) × ln(5.98H/(0.8W+T))

Embedded microstrip:
  εeff(H1) = εr − (εr − εeff,surface) × exp(−2.0·H1/H)
  Z0       = Z0,surface × √(εeff,surface / εeff(H1))

Symmetric stripline:
  Z0 = (60/√εr) × ln(1.9B/(0.8W+T)),  εeff = εr

Asymmetric stripline:
  Heff = 2·H1·H2/(H1+H2),  Beff = 2·Heff
  Z0   = (60/√εr) × ln(1.9·Beff/(0.8W+T))

Differential microstrip:
  Zdiff = 2·Z0 × [1 − 0.48·exp(−0.96·S/H)]

Differential stripline:
  Zdiff = 2·Z0 × [1 − 0.347·exp(−2.9·S/B)]

CPWG:
  k = W/(W+2G),  Z0,cpw = (30π/√εeff,cpw) / [K(k)/K′(k)]
  Z0 = [Z0,cpw × Z0,bottom-plane] / [Z0,cpw + Z0,bottom-plane]

Propagation delay (all geometries):
  tPD = √εeff / c   (c = speed of light)
  Velocity factor = 1/√εeff

The live width/height/spacing sweeps live in js/simulations.js, and the Plotly chart builders live in js/charts.js.

20. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the form only show some of the input fields?

Each geometry only uses a subset of the possible dimensions (for example, stripline uses total plane spacing B instead of dielectric height H) — the form shows only the fields relevant to your selected geometry, and updates automatically when you change it.

Is this calculator accurate enough for a real design?

It uses standard closed-form Hammerstad-Jensen / IPC-2141-style approximations, accurate enough for early stack-up planning and design review. Always confirm final trace geometry against your fab house's field-solver / impedance-control service before release.

Why doesn't my differential impedance exactly equal 2× the single-ended Z0?

Coupling between the two traces of a differential pair lowers the odd-mode impedance below the isolated single-ended value — the calculator applies a coupling factor based on spacing to approximate this, and it only approaches 2×Z0 as spacing becomes very large.

Can I save my calculator inputs and come back later?

Yes — use Save config in the Reports & Export card, then Load config on a future visit.

21. Troubleshooting

Charts are not rendering

Charts require Plotly.js from cdn.plot.ly — verify your network/firewall allows the CDN. The calculator, cross-section diagram, and decision tree work without it.

The narrator doesn't speak

Some browsers (or OS configurations) do not support the SpeechSynthesis API, or have no voices installed. The narrator widget still displays the script text so the lesson remains usable.

The tour doesn't highlight the expected section

Ensure you have not manually removed or renamed the section IDs (#tour-*) — the tour looks up each step by CSS selector and skips to the next step (or ends) if a target is missing.

22. Glossary

Z0
Characteristic impedance of a single-ended transmission line, √(L′/C′).
Zdiff
Differential impedance of a coupled pair.
εeff
Effective dielectric constant — the blended value a wave actually experiences.
εr
Bulk relative permittivity of the laminate.
tPD
Propagation delay per unit length, √εeff / c.
Velocity factor
Propagation velocity as a fraction of the speed of light.
CPWG
Grounded coplanar waveguide.
TDR
Time-Domain Reflectometry.
