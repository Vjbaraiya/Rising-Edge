1. Introduction

The PDN Optimizer is a frequency-domain exploration tool for selecting and evaluating decoupling capacitors. It calculates a rail's target impedance, models each capacitor type as a series RLC branch, combines enabled branches using complex admittance, adds common PCB/package parasitics, and compares the result with the target across a logarithmic frequency sweep.

Scope: This is a first-order engineering model. Final sign-off should use effective capacitance under DC bias, tolerance and aging, vendor impedance/S-parameter data, VRM and package models, and extracted layout parasitics.
Electrical requirements→Target impedance→Capacitor RLC bank→Frequency response→Recommendations

2. Quick Start (5–10 minutes)

  1. Open the optimizer and enter rail voltage.
  2. Enter the maximum transient load-current step.
  3. Enter allowable ripple as a percentage of rail voltage.
  4. Review the automatic target impedance and design margin.
  5. Set start/stop frequencies and analysis points.
  6. Enter estimated mounting, plane, and package parasitics.
  7. Use Auto mode to search the included library, or Manual mode to add capacitor values, ESR, ESL, package, and quantity.
  8. Select Optimize capacitor network or Simulate circuit.
  9. Inspect the green combined curve against the red target line, then review peaks and recommendations.
  10. Save the configuration or export CSV/JSON and print the report.
Fastest first run: Select Load example, switch to Manual, and simulate the preset.

5. Capacitor Network

Library fields

  • Capacitance (F): nominal charge-storage value.
  • ESR (Ω): dissipative resistance, affecting minimum impedance and damping.
  • ESL (H): part inductance, combined with mounting inductance.
  • Quantity: identical parallel parts; branch impedance is divided by quantity.
  • Package: physical-size identifier and an important clue to parasitic inductance.
  • Manufacturer: optional metadata for custom parts.

Manual controls

Add selects a library part. Custom accepts base SI units. Enable/disable supports A/B comparisons without deleting a row. Duplicate increases quantity. Remove deletes the selected type; Clear removes the entire manual bank.

Unit trap: Custom capacitance is entered in farads and ESL in henries. For example, 10 µF = 0.00001 F and 1 nH = 0.000000001 H.

6. Calculation Method

Target impedance

Ztarget = (VDD × ripple fraction / ΔI) × (1 − margin fraction).

Series RLC branch

Zcap = ESR + jω(ESL + Lmount) + 1/(jωC). For N identical capacitors, the branch impedance is divided by N.

Parallel combination

Ytotal = Σ(1/Zn), then Zparallel = 1/Ytotal. Plane resistance and jω(Lplane + Lpackage) are added as common series terms.

Self resonance

FSRF = 1/(2π√(LC)). Below SRF a branch is predominantly capacitive; above SRF it becomes inductive.

Anti-resonance and margin

Local peaks significantly higher than adjacent minima are reported as anti-resonance. Margin = −20 log10(Zworst/Ztarget). Positive dB passes; negative dB fails.

Optimizer

The greedy search identifies the current worst-frequency region and adds the library component whose SRF is closest, with a small penalty for repeatedly selecting the same type. It stops when the target is met or a search limit is reached.

Hover for exact values. Drag to zoom. Use the Plotly mode bar to zoom, pan, or reset. Select legend items to isolate contributions. A good response remains below target throughout the required band and avoids narrow peaks with inadequate tolerance margin.

8. Results & Recommendations

Target status

Target met means every sampled point passes. Increase analysis resolution around suspicious peaks before relying on a narrow margin.

Worst impedance and margin

Worst impedance is the maximum in-band response. Positive margin is headroom; negative margin quantifies the breach.

Devices and effective C

Device count sums enabled quantities. Effective C is currently the nominal parallel sum; manually enter derated C for realistic sign-off.

Selected capacitor matrix

Use SRF sorting to verify that selections cover the intended frequency span. A gap between adjacent SRFs may correspond to an anti-resonance region.

Recommended actions

  • Low-frequency breach: increase effective bulk C or improve regulator interconnect.
  • High-frequency breach: reduce shared/package/mounting inductance and improve placement.
  • Anti-resonance breach: add an intermediate value or controlled damping.
  • High via inductance: use short, wide connections and vias at capacitor pads.

11. Engineering Concepts

PDN and decoupling

The PDN includes regulator, board, capacitors, vias, planes, package, die, and return path. Decoupling capacitors locally provide transient charge because source current cannot change instantly through inductive interconnect.

ESR, ESL, and Q

ESR dissipates energy and controls the minimum near SRF. ESL limits high-frequency behavior. Very low loss can increase resonance Q; strategically controlled ESR can damp peaks.

Bulk, mid-frequency, and high-frequency hierarchy

Bulk capacitors support slower demand, mid-value MLCCs bridge the middle band, and small low-ESL packages address fast board-level currents. Physical loop area should generally shrink toward the load.

Placement

Put small high-frequency parts closest to power pins with one power and one ground via at the pads. Place bulk near the source/load region it supports. Avoid long traces before vias.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring DC-bias derating and tolerance.
  • Modeling parts but not the shared current path.
  • Adding many identical parts without bandwidth analysis.
  • Stopping the sweep below relevant edge content—or extending it beyond model validity.
  • Accepting a nominal pass with no manufacturing or temperature margin.

12. Worked Example

For a 1.0 V rail, 10 A step, 3% ripple, and 10% design margin:

Ztarget = (1.0 × 0.03 / 10) × 0.90 = 0.0027 Ω = 2.7 mΩ.

  1. Sweep 10 kHz to 1 GHz at 1200 points.
  2. Start with 0.5 nH mounting inductance.
  3. Load the manual preset and simulate.
  4. Identify the worst-frequency region and any marked peaks.
  5. If the low end fails, increase effective bulk C. If the high end fails, reduce ESL or add a small nearby MLCC.
  6. If a narrow peak fails, introduce an intermediate value or damping rather than blindly multiplying the existing values.
  7. Export CSV to compare variants and save the chosen configuration.

13. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is impedance above target?

The requirement may be aggressive, the bank may lack coverage, or shared R/L may dominate.

Which values should I choose?

Choose by required frequency coverage, effective capacitance, ESR/ESL, package, bias, tolerance, availability, and physical placement—not value alone.

Why are resonance peaks appearing?

Unlike capacitors and inductive paths exchange stored energy. Low damping makes the resulting anti-resonance sharp.

Why doesn't adding more always help?

More identical parts lower a branch but do not remove common inductance or automatically fill another frequency region.

How do I reduce anti-resonance?

Add an intermediate value, introduce appropriate ESR/damping, reduce connection inductance, or adjust quantities.

Why does placement matter?

The real current loop adds inductance. A good schematic capacitor connected through a long loop may be ineffective at high frequency.

16. Glossary

PDN
The complete power and return network between source and load.
Decoupling / bypass capacitor
Local energy storage providing a low-impedance transient-current path.
ESR
Equivalent series resistance; dissipative portion of the capacitor model.
ESL
Equivalent series inductance of component and connection.
Ripple voltage
Variation around nominal rail voltage.
Target impedance
Maximum PDN impedance allowed by ripple and transient-current requirements.
Self-resonant frequency (SRF)
Frequency at which capacitive and inductive reactance cancel.
Anti-resonance
Parallel-network impedance peak caused by interacting branches.
Power plane
Broad conductor distributing supply current.
Return path
Physical path carrying current back to its source.
Q factor
A measure of resonance sharpness and stored-to-dissipated energy.
Damping
Energy loss that lowers resonance amplitude.
